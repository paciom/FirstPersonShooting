A 30-second cinematic episode of an epic space-exploration saga, awe-inspiring and breathtaking, like a nature documentary crossed with a sci-fi epic. This is an ALIEN place — nothing about it should resemble Earth.

The hero: the orange, white and black knight robot with cyan trim from the first reference image — an angular horned helm with glowing cyan eyes, heavy black gauntlets and boots; its fighter-jet form is shown from several angles in the following reference images — note the sharp pointed nose at the front and the engine exhausts at the rear. LIGHTING RULE: the only light is the Ember — one small gold-white sphere hanging at the dead centre of the hollow world, no bigger than a hill, throwing long hard shadows outward along every needle. Consistent light direction and shadows in every shot. No moons.

FLIGHT RULE: whenever the jet flies, it always flies NOSE-FIRST — the sharp pointed nose leads in the direction of travel, engines trailing blue fire. It banks and carves like a real aircraft, never drifting sideways or backwards.

THE WORLD — the Needle Hollow: a hollow world whose entire inner surface has grown inward. From every part of the shell — under the flyer, beside it, and far overhead — colossal black mineral needles rise toward the centre, tapering from bases wider than mountains to tips finer than a tower, their flanks veined with cooling orange seams. Because the world is inside out, the needles point at each other from all directions, and their tips very nearly meet in a tight sphere of empty air around the Ember at the middle. There is no horizon: look any way at all and you see the far side's needles hanging down at you.

PHYSICS RULE: down means outward, toward the shell each needle is rooted in. The needles are still growing, so they creak, split and occasionally snap near the tip; a broken tip falls back down its own needle toward the shell, but a tip that breaks free at the very centre drifts weightless there instead, turning slowly in the light of the Ember. Nothing ever falls toward the middle.
