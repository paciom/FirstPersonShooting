A 30-second cinematic episode of an epic space-exploration saga, awe-inspiring and breathtaking, like a nature documentary crossed with a sci-fi epic. This is an ALIEN place — nothing about it should resemble Earth.

The hero: the white-and-orange robot with the black-and-white helmet and the wide red visor band from the first reference image — orange wing-fins on its shoulders and wheels built into its legs; its fighter-jet form is shown from several angles in the following reference images — note the sharp pointed nose at the front and the engine exhausts at the rear. The golden glyph cube artifact is the last reference image.

LIGHTING RULE: one dim gold sun lights everything; consistent light direction and shadows in every shot. No moons.

FLIGHT RULE: whenever the jet flies, it always flies NOSE-FIRST — the sharp pointed nose leads in the direction of travel, engines trailing orange fire. It banks and carves like a real aircraft, never drifting sideways or backwards.

THE WORLD — the Yearfall: a world where time falls out of the sky. Slow drifting motes of pale light rain down from a high golden haze, and every mote carries decades — whatever it lands on ages instantly. The result is a landscape of savage contrasts: wherever the fall is thick, the ground is a desert of collapsed, wind-eaten ruin and drifts of grey powder; wherever a great overhang shelters the land, the rock beneath is sharp, wet and brand new. Between them stand pillars sliced into two halves by a shadow line — one side pristine, one side crumbled to nothing.

TIME RULE: the falling motes only age what they touch, and they fall slowly and straight down, so shadow is safety. The robot loses years for every mote that lands on it — its plating scours, its colors fade — but under an overhang it takes no damage at all. Crossing open ground is therefore a race: the faster it moves, the fewer motes can land on it.

OPENING (0-3s): deep space. The robot drifts in orbit above a dull gold world whose entire day side is veiled in a slow shimmering fall of light, and beneath the veil the surface is visibly patched — bright new terrain in the shadowed valleys, ancient dead dust everywhere the fall reaches. Nothing about it resembles Earth. A small golden holographic counter floats beside its helmet reading exactly "FRAGMENT 006 / 512". The robot dives.

THE DIVE (3-16s): mid-fall the robot transforms into its fighter-jet form and drops nose-first through the golden haze, motes streaming over the canopy and leaving grey age-scars along the wings. It dives into a deep shadowed gorge where the rock is glass-sharp and untouched, flying low where the fall cannot reach; it bursts out across an open flat and instantly the fall thickens, its hull greying as it accelerates; it carves through a field of the half-eaten pillars, one flank of each pillar perfect and the other dissolving into drifting powder; then it climbs and the landmark appears — the PARASOL, a single vast natural shelf of rock a kilometer across, and beneath its shadow a perfect circle of brand-new land, sharp and gleaming, in the middle of a desert of ruin.

THE CHALLENGE (16-23s): the jet transforms back into the robot at the edge of the ruin, with the Parasol's shadow far across open ground. Halfway across, a thick curtain of the fall drifts into its path and it takes the full weight of it — its legs seize with age and it goes down hard, skidding. Setback. It hauls itself into the narrow shadow of a leaning pillar, shakes the powder off, waits for the curtain to drift, then explodes out and covers the last stretch flat-out into the Parasol's shade.

The glyphs flare — and for one breathtaking beat the entire Yearfall stops: every mote in the sky halts in place, an ocean of suspended light from horizon to horizon, and then rises gently back up into the haze, leaving the whole ruined plain standing clean and still.
