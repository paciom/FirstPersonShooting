A 30-second cinematic episode of an epic space-exploration saga, awe-inspiring and breathtaking, like a nature documentary crossed with a sci-fi epic. This is an ALIEN place — nothing about it should resemble Earth.

The hero: the white-and-orange robot with the black-and-white helmet and the wide red visor band from the first reference image — orange wing-fins on its shoulders and wheels built into its legs; its fighter-jet form is shown from several angles in the following reference images — note the sharp pointed nose at the front and the engine exhausts at the rear. LIGHTING RULE: one violet sun lights everything; consistent light direction and shadows in every shot. No moons.

FLIGHT RULE: whenever the jet flies, it always flies NOSE-FIRST — the sharp pointed nose leads in the direction of travel, engines trailing orange fire. It banks and carves like a real aircraft, never drifting sideways or backwards.

THE WORLD — the Loop Basin: a titanic bowl of grey stone where the land is caught in repeating loops of time, each loop a different length. In one basin a colossal natural arch collapses in a roar of dust, then snaps whole again and collapses, over and over, every few seconds. In another, a geyser of black liquid erupts, arcs, falls, and rewinds back into the ground. In another, a plateau splits open and closes like a wound. Dozens of loops of different lengths run side by side across the basin, so from above the whole landscape flickers and stutters in overlapping rhythms, a machine made of repeating disasters.

TIME RULE: every loop is perfectly regular and always repeats exactly the same way — the same collapse, the same eruption, the same instant of danger, at the same interval. Nothing that enters a loop is rewound with it, so the robot stays itself; it simply has to learn each rhythm and cross during the safe part of the cycle, and the short loops leave almost no safe window at all.

OPENING (0-3s): deep space. The robot drifts in orbit above a grey-violet world whose surface twitches — plumes of dust puffing up and sucking back down in the same places, again and again, like a broken recording playing across a whole continent. Nothing about it resembles Earth. A small golden holographic counter floats beside its helmet reading exactly "FRAGMENT 006 / 512". The robot dives.

THE DIVE (3-16s): mid-fall the robot transforms into its fighter-jet form and drops nose-first into the basin. It shoots the collapsing arch in the exact second it stands whole, dust from the last collapse still hanging as the stone re-forms around it; it banks through a forest of black geysers erupting and un-erupting in sequence, threading the gaps like a slalom; it skims a plateau that splits open beneath it and slams shut behind its exhaust; then it climbs and the landmark is revealed — the METRONOME, a vast stone ring at the basin's heart that shatters into a thousand pieces and reassembles once per second, faster and faster the closer you get, its edge blurred by the sheer rate of the loop.

On the third ring it mistimes by a fraction and the stone re-forms around its leg, pinning it as the next collapse begins. Setback. It waits one beat for the rewind to release it, then launches into a flat-out sprint and takes the last three rings in a single run without slowing.
