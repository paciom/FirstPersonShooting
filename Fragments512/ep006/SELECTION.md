# Episode 006 — RACER — domain: BROKEN TIME

Ten candidate worlds, all in `candidates/`.

1. **cand_01 — the Chronocline.** A rift valley where time speed changes
   continuously along its length: a violet lightning strike frozen mid-air at
   one end, glass-stemmed growths sprouting and crumbling to dust every second
   at the other; the cube sits inside the frozen bolt.
2. **cand_02 — the Countercurrent.** A canyon world running backwards —
   channels of liquid silver climb the walls in reversed cataracts, landslides
   leap back into place; the cube must be snatched out of a boulder that is
   busy un-shattering around it.
3. **cand_03 — the Echo Flats.** Black glass plains where every past event
   still replays as translucent ghosts that turn briefly SOLID on the beat of
   the original moment; the cube flickers into existence each time a ghost
   sets it down.
4. **cand_04 — the Timetide.** A copper plain crossed by visible moving waves
   of fast time: crystal spires grow, age and collapse in the seconds a wave
   takes to pass, and only something matching the wavefront's speed rides it
   unharmed; the cube is in a socket at the top of a tower that is rebuilt and
   eroded by every wave.
5. **cand_05 — the Stack.** Time layered by altitude — stone pillars pristine
   at the base and eroded to thread-thin needles at the summit, a sky tearing
   past overhead; every second spent high costs years, and the cube is on the
   very top.
6. **cand_06 — the Held Breath.** A hemisphere where time stopped in the
   middle of an eruption: magma hanging at the top of its arc, ash walls
   standing, the robot the only moving thing — and stopping means becoming
   part of the tableau; the cube crowns the frozen fire-fountain.
7. **cand_07 — the Loop Basin.** Dozens of side-by-side loops of different
   lengths, each replaying one disaster forever — an arch collapsing and
   re-forming, geysers rewinding; the cube blinks in and out on a pedestal
   that resets once a heartbeat.
8. **cand_08 — the Yearfall.** Time falls as slow motes of light that age
   whatever they touch, so shadow is safety and open ground is a race; the
   cube waits under a kilometer-wide rock shelf on land that has never known a
   single year.
9. **cand_09 — the Seam.** One hemisphere runs forward, one backward, mirrored
   across a pole-to-pole line of white light; the robot must run the Seam
   itself to the top of an arch that is eternally half-building and
   half-collapsing.
10. **cand_10 — the Afterworld.** Salt-white flats where speed sheds solid,
    expiring copies of your own past positions; the robot has to circle at
    full speed to build a bridge of its own past-selves across a chasm.

## Pick: **cand_04 — the Timetide**

Chosen because its strangeness is a *moving object you can point at*: a
shimmering wall sweeping across the plain with a whole generation of crystal
spires being born, weathering and collapsing in its wake, all inside one shot —
no narration needed, and it reads even on a phone-sized vertical frame. The
challenge exists nowhere else: the tower is only tall enough to climb at the
crest of a wave, and the only protection from a wave's thousand-fold time rate
is to travel exactly as fast as its leading edge, which makes this Racer's
episode specifically rather than any robot's — the setback (slipping into the
wave's body and instantly greying with age) and the recovery (matching the
wavefront's speed again, corrosion stopping mid-burn) are both pure speed
problems. The fragment beat is the strongest in the set alongside cand_06: every
wave on the planet halts mid-sweep and a horizon-wide field of spires stands
finished and perfect for the first time, which rhymes directly with the series'
promise of rebirth. cand_06 (the Held Breath) was the runner-up and is the
better single image, but ep001 already spent a whole episode on a frozen
landscape, and a second "everything is motionless" world this early would blunt
both; cand_10 was the most inventive challenge but risks the model rendering a
crowd of duplicate heroes, against the "robot always crisp and readable" rule.
