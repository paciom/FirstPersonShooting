THE WORLD — the Flare Coast: a burnt world orbiting so close to its star that the star is scenery. Its day side is a dark plain bristling with SPIRES — slender fused conductors kilometres tall, grown upward by ten million years of being struck. Every minute the star throws a FLARE TIDE: a rolling wall of charged fire that sweeps across the plain, and the spires answer it in a chain of colossal discharges, one after another to the horizon, before the sky darkens back to smoulder. Between the spires the ground is fused, black and glassy-smooth, veined with cooling violet light.

TIDE RULE: the flare tide always rolls in the same direction, from the star toward the night side, and always drains itself down the spires. The spires warn: they brighten and hum from base to tip a few seconds before the tide reaches them. Nothing in the open survives the sweep; anything that can carry the charge safely to the ground does.
