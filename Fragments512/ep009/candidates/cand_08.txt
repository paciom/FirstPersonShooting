A 30-second cinematic episode of an epic space-exploration saga, awe-inspiring and breathtaking, like a nature documentary crossed with a sci-fi epic. This is an ALIEN place — nothing about it should resemble Earth.

The hero: RANGER, the white robot with the rounded helmet and the glowing cyan visor, cyan-lit panels set across chunky white armor, from the first reference image; its fighter-jet form is shown from several angles in the following reference images — note the sharp pointed nose at the front and the engine exhausts at the rear. The golden glyph cube artifact is the last reference image.

LIGHTING RULE: one distant blue-white sun lights everything; consistent light direction and shadows in every shot. No moons.

FLIGHT RULE: whenever the jet flies, it always flies NOSE-FIRST — the sharp pointed nose leads in the direction of travel, engines trailing blue fire. It banks and carves like a real aircraft, never drifting sideways or backwards.

THE WORLD — THRUM, the thinking planet: one single organism made of filament, spanning the globe. Deep chasms of bare grey rock are bridged by immense woven cables of grey-green living fibre, tens of thousands of them, braided into causeways, hanging webs and towers. Signals run along the filaments as travelling green sparks — thoughts crossing continents — and where thoughts converge the fibres knot together into new structures and unravel old ones. The world has no water, no dust, no growth on the ground: only bare rock below and a thinking web above.

MIND RULE: this world thinks by rebuilding itself, and it thinks in seconds. Filament bridges weave themselves into existence a few metres ahead of anything crossing, and unravel a few metres behind it. Green thought-sparks race constantly along every cable and cluster around whatever the world is paying attention to. It is curious, never cruel — its bridges appear as often as they vanish.

OPENING (0-3s): deep space. RANGER drifts in orbit above a bare grey planet wrapped in a glowing web of green filaments that visibly rearranges as it watches, sparks racing along the strands in branching patterns — visibly NOT Earth. A small golden holographic counter floats beside its helmet reading exactly "FRAGMENT 009 / 512". The robot dives.

THE DIVE (3-16s): mid-fall RANGER transforms into its fighter-jet form and drops nose-first. A sweeping continuous flyover: threading between hanging filament curtains that part just before the nose and re-knit behind the exhausts; diving into a chasm where cable causeways stack floor above floor and green sparks race the jet along the rails; rolling through a hoop of fibre that ties itself shut in the mirror; then climbing to reveal the landmark — THE KNOT, a slowly rotating sphere of a million braided filaments the size of a mountain, hanging between two cliff walls, thought-sparks pouring into it from every direction. Fast, fluid, majestic — no slow motion.

THE CHALLENGE (16-23s): RANGER transforms back into the robot and runs out along a cable toward the Knot as the world weaves the walkway ahead of it and unweaves it behind. Halfway across the world changes its mind: the strand ahead unravels into empty air. RANGER leaps, catches a loose filament with one hand and swings over the chasm; the fibre stretches, thins — then a dozen sparks converge and the world braids a new cable around its arm and lifts it up onto the Knot's surface.

THE FRAGMENT (23-28s): the Knot has grown a chamber around the thing it cannot understand. Inside, held in a cradle of a thousand filament tips, is the artifact from the reference image — a single fist-sized golden metallic cube etched with glowing circuit glyphs, rising on a column of light as the fibres release it into RANGER's hands. The glyphs flare — and the whole planet answers: every filament on the world turns gold at once, and one enormous pattern of light writes itself across the entire web from horizon to horizon, a thought big enough to see from orbit.
