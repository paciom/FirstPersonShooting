The game

A whole league of robots,
and one tab to run it in.

Every hero is also a vehicle

Tap T mid-fight and your robot folds down into a hovercraft, a rail bike, a six-wheeled tank — whatever its build says it is. Drive it, then stand back up shooting.

The sky keeps dropping loot

Robots start with two guns. Every few seconds a crate parachutes into the arena with one of the other fifty-two inside — so a fight is really a race to whatever just landed.

Nobody ever gets hurt

Hits drain an energy shield. Lose it and your robot de-rezzes into light, then re-materializes for the next round. There is no blood, no gore and no death in this game — that is the fiction, not a euphemism.

Thirteen ways to play

Pick a mode. They are all in the same box.

ARENA COMBAT

AI v AI

Two robot teams fight it out on their own while a director camera cuts between them. Sit back — it's a broadcast.

Player v AI

You and three cyan allies against the magenta hunters. First person, shields up, airdrops incoming.

Online PvP

Share a room code with a friend and fight them browser to browser. Nothing to install on either end.

Brawl

Side-on, health bars up top, a real fighting game underneath: combos, blocks, throws and knockdowns.

Brawl: AI War

The same fighting game with the AI throwing the punches. Best of five, and you just call it.

Martial Arts Show

No opponent, no clock. Five robots run the entire move set as choreography under the lights.

WAR ROOM

Commander

Zoom out and run the war: raise the base, field the squads, push the map. Same robots, one hundred metres higher up.

Commander: AI War

Two commanders, both AI. A full strategy match plays itself out while you watch it happen.

Tower Defense

Raiders pour out of a warp gate and march the canyon. Socket your towers, spend well, hold the line.

Tank Raid

Your hero's chassis, a scrolling battlefield, and a great deal of incoming. Twin-stick, full throttle.

ACADEMY · WORKSHOP

Chinese Quest

One character, four robots holding four meanings. Choose, and your hero crosses the stage to settle it — every answer spoken aloud in Mandarin.

Chinese Run

The same question at a dead sprint. The road is the clock: run out of it and you have answered wrong.

Arena Builder

Fly the arena and reroll the cover until it is the map you wanted. It stays there for the next match.

The roster

Nine heroes. Nine second forms.

Point at any of them to watch it transform. Every one of these is the real in-game robot, rendered by the game itself.

One button

Robot. Vehicle.
Back again, at speed.

Transforming is not a cutscene — it happens where you are standing, in the middle of whatever you were doing. Chase somebody down as a speeder, unfold into a robot behind them, and finish the argument on foot.

Every hero carries a vehicle shaped like its own build: Ranger's hovercraft is light and pointed, Titan's assault tank is six wheels of armour, Racer is a formula car with a nose cone. Nine robots, nine vehicles, one key.

For parents and teachers

Built for 8–14, and it shows.

Justice Armored Heroes was made for a kid who wants robots and a grown-up who would rather not supervise a shooter. Both of those are true at once here.

  • No violence to people. The cast is robots, the damage is an energy shield, and a beaten robot dissolves into light and comes back.
  • No strangers. There is no public matchmaking and no chat. Online play needs a room code shared between two people who already know each other.
  • No accounts, no data. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to verify, no email asked for. Progress lives in the browser.
  • No money. No purchases, no currency, no loot boxes, no ads. The whole game is the whole game.
  • It teaches, if you want it to. Two of the thirteen modes drill Chinese characters — 2,000 of them, with spaced repetition and real Mandarin pronunciation.
  • It runs on the laptop you already have. A browser is the only requirement.
